Noise Generator

Generate procedural textures with Perlin, Simplex, Value, Worley and White noise. Build height, normals, albedo, roughness and specular maps. Inspect the material under light, then export up to 4K.

§ 01

Surface settings

PROCEDURAL
Albedo, roughness & specular settings

Albedo blends two colors in linear space and exports sRGB, without baked light. Specular is linear dielectric F0 (reflectance), separate from roughness; 0.04 is a common starting point. This is not Unreal’s remapped Specular scalar.

Macro controls broad shape; the independent micro layer adds fine detail. Anisotropy stretches the surface along Direction. Normal strength changes normals without changing height.

§ 02

Texture & material

512 PX PREVIEW

The preview interprets normals as OpenGL internally. Export uses your selected convention. Material lighting uses the generated albedo, roughness and specular. Roughness and reflectance are artist-controlled ranges, not measured material properties.

§ 03

Export maps

PNG / ZIP

ZIP includes Height, Normal DX, Normal GL, Roughness, Albedo, Specular and settings JSON with color-space information. All PNGs are 8-bit per channel, square, with identical seed and coordinates. Full resolution is generated at export; 4K uses more memory and time.

§ 04

How it works

Choose a preset, tune the structure, then inspect it on the sphere or tiled plane. Height Noise produces a scalar texture for masks and bump. Normal Noise combines primary and micro surface normals using Reoriented Normal Mapping.

Normals use central differences in UV space with a reference height of 0.02 UV units. Seamless mode wraps sampling and smoothing; oriented surfaces blend periodic domains. Perlin, Value, Worley and White use periodic lattice sampling. Simplex and oriented surfaces use a smooth periodic domain blend. Frequency is rounded to whole periods per octave in seamless mode. Edge texels are adjacent samples of a periodic signal, not duplicated borders.

Import normals as linear normal-map data in your engine. Choose DirectX or OpenGL to match its tangent convention. Height, roughness and specular are linear data; albedo uses sRGB. Specular exports dielectric F0 reflectance, not metalness or an engine-specific scalar. White noise has hard cell boundaries; smooth it before using it as surface normals.
