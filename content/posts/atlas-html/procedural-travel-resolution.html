01 — From an address to a destination

The previous article, Procedural Address Resolution, established what place a generated door represents. This article follows the next step: using that world coordinate to load an interior and reach the correct arrival point. The current project supports travel from the street into an entrance and into a specific apartment. Apartment travel was completed after the 4 October architecture audit and confirmed by the project author. This draft describes the architecture slice; media will provide the route demonstrations.

02 — Address identity and physical arrival

An address identifies a logical place. An anchor provides a physical arrival transform. World position is not the address: moving a generated door should update its associated arrival point rather than change the meaning of the coordinate. The same interior asset can represent different addressed locations. Reusing a layout does not make those apartments or entrances the same place.

03 — The Blueprint boundary

Procedural placements supply identity and metadata. MazePlacementExtras creates Portal and Anchor actors through placement rules. The address layer binds canonical context to those actors. BP_TravelManager resolves the request, makes the destination available, sets its BP_LocationRoot context and matches the arrival anchor. The travel manager does not need to interpret Houdini groups or draw address digits.

04 — Entering and returning

A street door carries a building and entrance identity. Its portal requests the entrance destination. After the destination is available, the travel manager passes the coordinate to its LocationRoot and moves the player to the matching anchor. The return path resolves an outside anchor associated with that specific entrance. A generic street destination would lose the relationship to the original door. Apartment travel extends the same relationship to a full building / entrance / apartment coordinate. A neighbouring apartment remains a distinct destination even when both use the same interior asset.

05 — What went wrong along the way

An anchor created in a street level was initially assigned the street identity even when its purpose was arrival in an entrance. Logical destination must follow the role of the anchor, not the level in which its source actor happened to appear. A mismatched apartment-door HISM selector also prevented rules from creating travel actors. Correcting the source binding produced portals and return anchors for the generated doors. Rebuilding must not accumulate duplicate actors. Creation order is another boundary: procedural actors may register before addresses are ready. Pending registration and a reconciliation pass avoid making successful configuration depend on which actor appeared first. The separate address article explains the JSON summary solution for apartment counts. Travel consumes the resulting coordinate rather than computing a fresh numbering policy.

06 — Evidence to add before publishing

  • Record two different entrance destinations and their street-side return points.
  • Record two neighbouring apartment destinations and their exact return doors.
  • Repeat visits in a different order and inspect updated LocationRoot context.
  • Rebuild procedural extras and confirm no duplicate portals or anchors.
  • Inspect failed requests for cleared pending state and no stale arrival anchor.

These are recording and review cases. Website preview checks do not test the Unreal travel implementation.

07 — Next iterations

Floors are the next planned spatial subdivision. A larger library of entrance, apartment and room variants should make exploration less repetitive. The aim is several hundred procedural presets in a later iteration, not a claim about the current catalog. Logical address and selected representation remain separate: a variation describes how a space looks, while its coordinate describes which place it is.

08 — Result

Address resolution answers “what place is this?” Travel resolution answers “how do I load and reach it?” Keeping those questions separate lets generated locations share reusable assets while retaining a specific identity and a specific return destination.
